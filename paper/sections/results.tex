\subsection{Generation versus selection}
\label{sec:res-formats}
Candidate selection beats free generation for every LLM. Accuracy rises from 8.1\% to 58.5\% for Qwen 7B, 11.5\% to 69.8\% for Qwen 14B, 50.7\% to 63.8\% for DictaLM (hand-judged generation), 54.3\% to 93.7\% for Gemini, 33.9\% to 83.2\% for GPT-4.1 mini, 43.3\% to 89.0\% for Claude Haiku and 48.3\% to 92.1\% for Grok. % TODO: one sentence on why, from the error analysis, e.g. where generated answers go wrong
Because selection is constrained to the inventory, this gain also depends on the inventory containing the right answer, which holds for every test item (the oracle is 100\%).

\subsection{Fine-tuning and the encoder}
\label{sec:res-encoder}
Fine-tuning raises the encoder from 64.3\% to 71.7\%, a gain of 7.3 points from fewer than 3,000 training items. The fine-tuned encoder beats every open LLM selection (at most 69.8\%) and every proprietary generation (at most 54.3\%). It trails the best proprietary selection (93.7\%) by 22.0 points. So a model about 35 times smaller than Qwen 7B can beat Qwen at selection, but it does not match the best system.

Scale alone does not explain these results. Doubling Qwen from 7B to 14B parameters raises selection from 58.5\% to 69.8\% and generation from 8.1\% to 11.5\%, so the extra 7B parameters help, mainly when candidates are given. DictaLM 2.0, a Hebrew-specialised model of 7.25B parameters, roughly 35 times larger than DictaBERT, reaches 63.8\% in selection. That is 7.9 points below the fine-tuned encoder and about the same as the untrained one (64.3\%). Hebrew pretraining therefore gives DictaLM the strongest open-model generation (50.7\%, judged by hand and so not strictly comparable with the automatic scores of the other models; 32.8\% under the automatic rule), but task-specific fine-tuning on fewer than 3,000 items matters more for selection than an LLM's size or Hebrew specialisation. We ran each system once, so we cannot say how stable these gains are, and the half-point gap between DictaLM and the untrained encoder is not meaningful.

\subsection{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}
We draw three conclusions. First, a small encoder fine-tuned on little data can beat a much larger LLM that is not fine-tuned. DictaBERT, with about 0.2B parameters and fewer than 3,000 mostly weakly labeled training items, reaches 71.7\% in selection, above the Hebrew-specialised DictaLM 2.0 (7.25B, 63.8\%) and both Qwen models. Fine-tuning adds 7.3 points over the untrained encoder, and earlier development runs showed gains in the same direction. A larger or cleaner training set would likely help further; we leave this to future work. The proprietary LLMs still lead the encoder by 11.5 to 22.0 points.

Second, choosing from candidates beats writing the expansion freely. Selection beats generation for all seven LLMs, by 13.1 points for DictaLM to 58.3 points for Qwen 14B. This assumes that the inventory contains the right answer, as it does for every test item.

Third, automatic scoring underrates free generation. Judged by hand, DictaLM's generation rises from 32.8\% to 50.7\%, moving it from below GPT-4.1 mini to above Grok. In the partial review of the other systems, 338 of 674 reviewed answers that the automatic rule marked wrong were judged correct. How generation is judged therefore changes which models look better, and the other generation scores, which remain automatic, are likely underestimates.
