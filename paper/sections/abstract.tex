Hebrew acronyms can have several meanings in context. How do free generation and selection from a supplied inventory compare, and how does a small task-trained Hebrew encoder compare with prompted language models? We study these questions on 381 test sentences, 77\% naturally occurring, comparing seven LLMs and two uses of DictaBERT. The inventory includes ordinary expansions and conventional interpretations such as gematria. Exact test acronym strings are absent from the documented train/dev files; historical training inputs and pretraining exposure are not fully known. In the main comparison, the task-trained encoder reaches 71.7\% in selection, competitive with local Qwen models (58.5--69.8\%), while API models score higher (83.2--93.7\%). Selection scores exceed generation scores for all six main LLMs. Candidate access and scoring differ together, so this gap does not isolate semantic ability. DictaLM is reported separately with an input-template limitation. The findings support candidate ranking as a practical local approach and show why answer format is central to evaluating acronym resolution.
