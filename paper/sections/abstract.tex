Hebrew acronyms are often ambiguous, and only context tells their meanings apart. We build a Hebrew dataset and evaluation pipeline that compares two ways of resolving an acronym on the same test occurrences: generating the expansion freely, or selecting it from a candidate list. We evaluate seven LLMs, three local and four API models, and DictaBERT, a 184M-parameter Hebrew encoder trained as a candidate ranker. The trained encoder reaches 71.7\% in selection, above Qwen 2.5 7B (58.5\%) and the Hebrew-specialised DictaLM 2.0 (63.8\%) and close to Qwen 2.5 14B (69.8\%), while the API models score higher (83.2--93.7\%). Selection scores higher than generation for every LLM under the automatic rules, by 31 to 58 points, although automatic string matching underrates generation. The data, saved model responses and analysis notebook are publicly available.
