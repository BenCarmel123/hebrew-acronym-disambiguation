Hebrew acronyms are often ambiguous, and only context tells their meanings apart. We build a Hebrew dataset and evaluation pipeline that compares two ways of resolving an acronym on the same 381 test occurrences: generating the expansion freely, or selecting it from a candidate list. We evaluate six main LLMs, two local and four API models, and DictaBERT, a 184M-parameter Hebrew encoder trained as a candidate ranker on fewer than 3,000 examples. The trained encoder reaches 71.7\% in selection, above Qwen 2.5 7B (58.5\%) and close to Qwen 2.5 14B (69.8\%), while the API models score higher (83.2--93.7\%). Selection beats generation for every LLM, by 39 to 58 points. Automatic string matching underrates generation: judging the Hebrew-specialised DictaLM 2.0's answers by hand raises its generation score from 32.8\% to 50.7\%. The data, saved model responses and analysis notebook are publicly available.
