\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}
\paragraph{Hebrew acronyms.}
HAADS disambiguates abbreviations in Hebrew and Aramaic Jewish Law documents \citep{hacohenkerner2010haads}. \citet{jacobs2020acronyms} build an acronym dictionary for Modern Hebrew from unannotated text and use context to choose among its entries. These works provide a dictionary-based context for our comparison. We study expansion in context, with a candidate list supplied only in the selection format.

\paragraph{Selecting from candidates.}
SciAD formulates acronym disambiguation as choosing a long form from a dictionary-derived candidate set given the sentence and acronym \citep{veyseh2020acronym}. \citet{chen2023gladis} introduce GLADIS, an English benchmark with acronym-disjoint splits. Their model, AcroBERT, reads the context and one candidate together and learns to tell matching pairs from non-matching ones. Our encoder follows this pair-scoring idea but uses binary cross-entropy, whereas AcroBERT uses a triplet ranking objective. GLADIS adapts two entity-disambiguation datasets by replacing full expressions with acronyms and re-splits the existing SciAD dataset. It therefore does not answer our generation--selection question for Hebrew naturally abbreviated text. We use mostly naturally occurring test sentences and keep substituted sentences for training.

\paragraph{Judging generated answers.}
\citet{agrawal2022large} ask GPT-3 to expand clinical abbreviations without choices, then map generated text to candidates by character overlap. \citet{meconi2025large} compare word-sense selection with generated definitions and explanations rated by an expert; strong free-form performance under that protocol motivates distinguishing string agreement from semantic evaluation. Neither study supplies directly comparable Hebrew acronym scores. Option positions and identifiers can also affect LLM choices \citep{zheng2024selection}. Our comparison preserves the two automatic rules and one candidate order, with selective human review used only to diagnose disagreements.
