\subsection{Supervised Hebrew candidate ranker}
DictaBERT is a pretrained Hebrew encoder \citep{shmidman2023dictabert}. Our implementation is a \emph{cross-encoder}: the marked sentence and one candidate are encoded jointly, allowing attention between their tokens. A dropout layer and a linear head turn the pooled representation into a scalar score. The model uses the initial classification token (CLS). Candidates are scored independently, and prediction chooses the largest score within the item's inventory.

For each training occurrence, the reference candidate is positive and the other supplied candidates are negative. The implemented objective is binary cross-entropy on the candidate-pair logits:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\big[&z_j\log\sigma(u_j)\,+\\
&(1-z_j)\log(1-\sigma(u_j))\big],
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where $M$ is the number of pairs, $u_j$ is a score, $\sigma$ is the sigmoid function and $z_j\in\{0,1\}$ is the label. Thus occurrences with larger inventories contribute more pairs. This is binary pair training, not a multiclass softmax over each inventory. AdamW updates the encoder and scoring head; no parameter-efficient adapter is used in this implementation.

The checkpoint is attributed by the authors to the Colab training procedure based on \texttt{dicta-il/dictabert}. That notebook specifies dropout 0.1, learning rate $2\times10^{-5}$, pair batch size 16, one epoch and seed 42. It sets the seed after model initialization, so initialization is not controlled by that setting. Checkpoints are selected by strict improvement in development pair loss, which differs from occurrence-level selection accuracy.

The pair layout places the marked context before the candidate, separated by BERT special tokens, with a nominal limit of 256 tokens. Context is cropped from the outside while retaining the target and candidate. Candidate length can affect the context budget; the LLMs receive the full sentence. The supplied checkpoint was saved in fp16 and loaded in full, including the encoder and scoring head, for fp32 development inference. Loading checks support reconstruction but do not authenticate the original training inputs or executed settings. The actual seed and complete training record remain unresolved; notebook settings above are not a verified execution log. Detailed loading evidence and remaining provenance gaps are documented in the repository.

\subsection{Prompted language models}
The generation prompt, written in Hebrew, asks for the expansion of the marked acronym in a few words, without explanation. The selection prompt supplies lettered candidates and requests exactly one letter. Neither contains demonstrations or identifies the correct answer. Full prompt templates belong to the technical companion; their information content and response requirements are the experimental distinction described here.

The development runs use \texttt{qwen2.5:7b} from the Qwen2.5 family \citep{qwen2025technical}, through Ollama with Q4\_K\_M quantization, and \texttt{gemini-3.8-flash} with thinking level \texttt{low}. Qwen's server digest is consistent across both tasks; successful Gemini responses preserve the returned version string. No additional Qwen decoding options were supplied, and omitted service defaults remain unresolved. These settings do not establish deterministic decoding; low thinking does not mean disabled thinking.

Requests have a 120-second timeout. The basic path has no automatic retry; Gemini continuation permits up to three attempts per item for transient errors, with backoff and saved progress. Completed responses are retained. The saved runs contain all 62 DictaBERT selections and both sets of 62 Qwen responses; Gemini coverage is partial after HTTP 429 and 503 errors. Prompts, candidate orders, raw answers, model identities and failure statuses are preserved in the \codebase{results/dev-2026-10-03/summary.json}{development records}.
