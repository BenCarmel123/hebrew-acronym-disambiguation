\section{Task and Data}
\label{sec:data}
\subsection{The unit of prediction}
An item is a tuple $(a_i,x_i,s_i,r_i,C_i,y_i)$: an acronym type, a sentence, a half-open character span, its exact raw text, a candidate inventory, and a reference expansion. The span identifies one occurrence even when the sentence contains several abbreviations. We preserve $r_i=x_i[s_i]$, including an attached prefix, and mark that span with \texttt{[ACR]} and \texttt{[/ACR]}. The type is a grouping key; it is not substituted for the raw target in the input.

Generation predicts a short expansion from $(x_i,s_i,r_i)$, without candidates or the reference. Selection predicts one member of $C_i$ from the same marked sentence and the supplied inventory. The reference label is used for supervised training and evaluation. Selection includes the reference among its candidates but does not identify which answer is correct. The intended answer is a linguistic expansion appropriate to the occurrence. An entity description or a plausible etymology alone is insufficient. Some named uses require an explicit annotation decision: for example, the current development labels accept \heb{רבי משה בן מימון} for \heb{רמב״ם}, including institutional uses. This is a recorded local convention, not a general rule for all names.

\subsection{Sources and supervision}
The retained collection draws on Hebrew Wikipedia and Knesset proceedings. Candidate evidence was collected from Wikipedia abbreviation and disambiguation pages and Wiktionary. Sentence and label sources are distinct: neither natural occurrence nor dictionary membership establishes the correct expansion in context.

Most training examples are \emph{substituted}: a spelled-out expansion in a Wikipedia sentence was replaced with the acronym, and the removed expression supplies a weak label. Natural examples retain an observed acronym; a small \emph{deglossed} subset removes an adjacent explanation. The current release also contains Claude-authored examples. Substitution and authorship supply supervision cheaply but need not reproduce naturally abbreviated text. Stored labels were reused without comprehensive reannotation.

All 62 development items are natural Knesset occurrences. For 42, preparation reuses content-matched historical review attributed to Ben; 20 carry Shaked's decisions made with AI assistance. This is not independent double annotation. Current training metadata marks 2,507 labels as not independently verified during preparation, 316 with historical review evidence and six with Shaked's AI-assisted decisions. These evidence categories, rather than a generic stored ``verified'' flag, describe the available review.

The saved qualification manifest still describes the earlier 2,665-item derivative. It cannot authenticate the expanded CSV or its separation guarantees. The \codebase{data/study_v1/encoder_inputs/train.csv}{versioned inputs} identify the inspected files; the paper build documentation records their hashes and counting definitions. Shaked identifies the supplied weights as Ben's current Colab training run; the state dictionary itself contains no training-input hash. Identity of the available CSV therefore does not establish the exact rows used during training.

\subsection{Qualification and separation}
The original preparation checked item identity, document provenance, target boundaries, duplicate text, candidate structure and split overlap. It located targets by a unique bounded match after limited quotation-mark folding, or by a recorded human span. Each included pair-loss item requires at least two distinct candidates and exactly one candidate matching the reference after surrounding whitespace is trimmed. Structural validity does not establish semantic correctness of labels or candidates.

In the current files, exact acronym-type sets and exact sentence strings do not overlap between train and dev. However, three nonempty document keys are shared, involving three training rows and five development rows. Thus the updated files do not support a claim of complete document separation. The original checks against reserved evaluation metadata predate the release and are not a fresh audit of it. No test content was inspected for this manuscript update. The recorded local rule excluding 11 otherwise eligible training occurrences of \heb{ד״ר} because \heb{בד״ר} occurs in dev addresses one prefix-related overlap; it does not establish separation of all morphological families.

Every development type has only one observed reference expansion; five of the 11 types have one item each. Several inventory candidates therefore do not imply several observed meanings. This sample supports development checks but cannot establish discrimination between senses of the same acronym or final-test generalization.

The current design differs from the submitted proposal in using a smaller natural development collection, adding Knesset text and emphasizing task-training type separation. The original document-first split, larger held-out target and broader baseline list are not presented as completed commitments. The final test population and inclusion rules still require an identified record from the authors.
