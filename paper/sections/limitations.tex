\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
\paragraph{Data and labels.} Natural and authored sentences have different construction and review histories, and one person reviewed the labels, so no inter-annotator agreement is available. The 41 Wikipedia test labels have no recorded review verdict, and 64 inventory entries were added while reviewing Knesset sentences, including test sentences. Three test references are wrong. \texttt{kn4-0299} and \texttt{nat-0041} concern overseas volunteers, not the political party recorded for \heb{מח״ל}. \texttt{kn4-0207} uses \heb{ס״ב} for page 62, not ``top secret''. We keep the original labels and report a correction check in Section~\ref{sec:res-encoder}. Variants of \heb{עכו״ם} remain unresolved. In development, at least two \heb{מט״ח} items about the educational technology center are labeled foreign currency, and \heb{רבי משה בן מימון} is accepted for \heb{רמב״ם} in hospital and street contexts.

\paragraph{Training records.} The encoder's training run was not fully logged: its exact input file, actual seed and selected epoch are unknown, and the similarity baseline's run record is also missing. The reference code sets its seed after model creation. An earlier source file, \path{data/splits/train_items.csv}, shares 60 Knesset protocols with 97 test items, and we cannot confirm which file the historical run used. Exposure of any model to the test sentences during pretraining is unknown.

\paragraph{Measurement.} Generation and selection use different automatic rules, so the gap between them does not isolate the effect of seeing candidates. The human review of the six LLMs other than DictaLM was blind to system identity, and the annotator had not seen AI evaluations of those responses. It is selective and judges agreement with the displayed reference, so it cannot rank systems by human accuracy. DictaLM's hand judgments follow a partly documented rule, it is the only model whose selection score depends on reading the leading label, and its chat template omits the publisher's newline after \texttt{[/INST]}, with unknown effect on its scores.

\paragraph{Comparison.} Each system ran once, with one prompt and one candidate order, and runs are not fully deterministic. Models differ in supervision, architecture, size and output budget, and low thinking is not disabled thinking. The similarity baseline differs from the trained ranker in architecture, so their gap is not a pure training effect. We measured no latency or memory costs.
