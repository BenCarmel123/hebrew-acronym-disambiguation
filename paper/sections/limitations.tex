\section{Limitations}
Training is dominated by substituted Wikipedia examples and includes AI-authored text, whereas development consists of natural parliamentary text. Domain, construction and type composition differ together. The current files also share three document keys across train and dev. Weak labels, unverified candidates, unmodeled morphological families and unknown pretraining exposure limit generalization claims. The missing original training metadata limits reproducibility despite the verified full-weight reconstruction.

Development contains only 62 items across 11 unevenly represented types, with one observed reference expansion per type. Five singleton types contribute five elevenths of macro accuracy, whereas the largest type contributes 15 of 62 items to micro accuracy. Neither summary alone establishes stable performance across types or contextual sense discrimination. Development also informs checkpoint selection and diagnostics.

Inspection exposed concrete development-label and inventory defects. One \heb{מט״ח} sentence explicitly denotes \mbox{\heb{המרכז לטכנולוגיה חינוכית}} (the educational technology center) but is labeled \mbox{\heb{מטבע חוץ}} (foreign currency). All nine \heb{חמ״ס} inventories omit \mbox{\heb{חומרים מסוכנים}} (hazardous materials), including contexts that spell it out; matching the stored label can therefore reward an unsuitable expansion. These observations can both depress and inflate label-based accuracy. They are findings about the inspected dev items, not evidence of the same defects in test.

Named uses require a separate distinction. The recorded decision accepts \mbox{\heb{רבי משה בן מימון}} for \heb{רמב״ם} even in hospital and street contexts. A hospital description may identify a referent without meeting that expansion convention. Disagreement on these items is therefore not automatically a labeling error, and a rule should not be changed retrospectively to favor a system.

Candidate access is coupled to response format, and the encoder and LLMs differ in supervision and architecture. Strict parsing may penalize a clear intended selection; an inventory may exclude a valid expansion. Partial service coverage and unjudged generation further limit comparisons. A training-majority baseline is undefined for task-unseen types, and no alternative baseline or uncertainty procedure has yet been adopted for final evaluation.
