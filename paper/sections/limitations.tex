\section{Limitations}
\paragraph{Data and labels.} The test set mixes natural and authored text, with different label-review evidence by source (Section~\ref{sec:data}). Domain, construction and acronym mix differ from the mostly substituted training data. The small development set also has label defects: one \heb{מט״ח} sentence about the educational technology center is labeled foreign currency, limiting checkpoint selection and development comparisons. Named uses follow local conventions: development accepts \heb{רבי משה בן מימון} for \heb{רמב״ם} even in hospital and street contexts. A stored reference is therefore not a complete account of valid interpretations. Exact-string separation in the documented files does not exclude related forms or establish absence from historical training or pretraining.

\paragraph{Measurement and comparison.} The substring generation rule can reject valid variants and accept longer answers containing the reference. The selective human review cannot estimate population accuracy or rank systems; DictaLM's full generation review uses a separately documented acceptance layer. Its template mismatch also limits comparison (Section~\ref{sec:eval}). Candidate access, response format and scoring change together, while models differ in supervision, architecture and size. These results concern one run per system, without variability estimates. Incomplete historical training records and similarity-run provenance further limit reproducibility. Broader claims require evaluation beyond these model configurations and a common semantic assessment of generated and selected answers.
